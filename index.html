About

She starts with the person

Families call when daily life is getting harder. A parent may be unsteady. A spouse may be worn out. The right next step is not always clear.

Suzanne asks what a good day looks like. She asks what feels unsafe. She asks who else is already helping. She wants to understand the person before talking about places or paperwork.

She does not replace a physician, an attorney, or the staff at a care community. She helps you speak plainly about what the older adult needs.

Elder care advocacy

Kinds of care people compare

These are common paths. They are not a list of providers. Suzanne does not choose one for you.

In-home care

Help that comes to the house. Someone may stay at home longer with support for meals, bathing, or company.

Independent living

A community for older adults who still manage most of the day on their own.

Assisted living

A residence with help for daily tasks, such as meals, dressing, or medicine reminders.

Memory care

A more structured setting for a person living with dementia.

Bring your questions. Suzanne helps you decide what to ask a clinician, an attorney, or a community.

Mobile notary

The notary comes to the signer

Many older adults cannot easily travel to a bank or a shipping store. A hospital stay or a move can come with forms that need a signature.

Suzanne is a mobile notary in Nashville. You agree on a time. You agree on a place you can both use. Mention any visitor rules if the meeting is at a facility.

Where you can ask to meet

  • A house or apartment
  • An office
  • A hospital
  • A nursing home or other care community

What to have ready

  • A current photo ID for each signer
  • The document, already prepared
  • Any witnesses the document itself requires

The signer must be alert, willing, and able to understand the paper. Suzanne checks identity and notarizes the signature. She does not draft wills, trusts, or powers of attorney. She does not give legal, medical, or financial advice.

Working together

How a conversation goes

  1. You tell her what has changed at home.
  2. She listens to concerns, preferences, and limits.
  3. You talk through in-home care, independent living, assisted living, and memory care in plain language.
  4. You leave with questions for the professionals you trust.
  5. If a form must be signed, you can ask for a notary visit in Nashville.
